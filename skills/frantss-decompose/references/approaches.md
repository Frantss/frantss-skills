# Explore alternatives when applicable

Alternatives apply when different approaches could materially change the outcome, effort, risk, or scope.
Compare credible approaches against the agreed goals and constraints; include doing nothing when relevant.
Consider a recommended approach and at least two credible alternatives when available.
When fewer credible alternatives fit, explain that limitation instead of padding the comparison.

Explain the recommendation and tradeoffs. Keep each approach proposed until frantss chooses or accepts a direction.
Complete this stage when the direction is agreed and the reasons for discarding alternatives are recorded.
Distinguish frantss's stated reasons from your reasoning for the accepted recommendation.

If alternatives do not apply, record why. If a direction is already agreed, retain it unless new evidence challenges its basis.
An agreed conclusion may be to make no change or gather more evidence; structure the remaining discussion around that outcome.
